Does opting out stop fingerprinting?

Separating tracking from security fingerprinting under verified consent and content blockers

149 popular sites · German VPN exit · 8 conditions · two crawls (22 & 25 Nov 2026)

TrackMeasure[team names]

The problem: same API call, two purposes

Ad-tech

Canvas read-back + attributes → recognise the visitor across sites. Needs consent (ePrivacy Art. 5(3); EDPB Guidelines 2/2023 cover fingerprinting).

Bot management

Same canvas read-back → decide whether the visitor is a human. Arguably strictly necessary for the service.

Detectors (Englehardt & Narayanan 2016, FP-Inspector 2021) say that a script fingerprints — not why.

Opt-out studies count all fingerprinting together, and often trust the click instead of the stored consent state.

A site that keeps fingerprinting after “reject all” may be breaking the rules — or protecting its checkout.

Motivation2

Research questions

RQ1

Can tracking fingerprinting be told apart from security fingerprinting, and how much of each is there?

RQ2

Does a verified consent rejection — with or without GPC — or GPC alone stop tracking fingerprinting and identifier sharing?

RQ3

Do content blockers stop it, and what else do they remove?

Starting reference: Iqbal, Englehardt & Shafiq, Fingerprinting the Fingerprinters (S&P 2021) — we keep its transparent rules and add purpose, verified consent and evidence that data leaves the browser.

Replication8

Can we trust the labels?

Behaviour agrees

96.5% of security scripts probe navigator.webdriver vs 70.8% of ad/analytics scripts (Fisher p = 3·10⁻⁷). Strong techniques: 87% vs 12%.

Independent sources

Vendor signatures vs Tracker Radar: κ = 0.45 on 11 domains (disagreements on secondary purpose).

Robustness

  • Re-assigning API calls by timestamp: 0.9% of calls move, 0 tracking pairs change on verified sites.
  • Frozen visit (host sleep) excluded: no change.
  • Noise floor: repeat baseline on the same sites.

80-script sample, 2 blind AI annotators

Haiku + Sonnet, no access to our labels or to each other.
Security labels confirmed 33/33 · tracking labels 11/14 · detector right on 41/43 decided scripts · purpose κ = 0.92.
A consistency check, not human ground truth.
Validity9

Limitations

  • API use ≠ proven tracking — attribute and identifier flows are direct but lower-bound evidence.
  • Labels are estimates — 27% of pairs stay Unknown (bounded by the upper bound); validated by two AI annotators, not humans; tracking precision rests on 14 scripts.
  • Scale — 97 reachable sites; the VPN’s data-centre IP is blocked by many e-commerce sites; consent comparisons rest on 31–56 verified sites.
  • Sequential conditions over ~7 h — noise floor + replication bound the drift; randomised order is future work.
  • One vantage point (Germany), home page + one subpage, no login/checkout; blocker breakage not measured.
  • We report candidates for non-compliance, not legal findings.
Limitations10

Take-aways

Separate by purpose. Two thirds of fingerprinting — and almost all strong fingerprinting — is bot & fraud management, often hidden behind first-party URLs.
Opt-outs act on cookies, not on fingerprinting. Tracking fingerprinting runs before consent and survives a verified “no”; identifiers keep flowing to trackers; GPC adds nothing.
Blockers work — with collateral. −86 pp tracking with uBlock, but anti-fraud scripts go too.

Next: human adjudication of the validation sample · randomise condition order · link fingerprint values to identifiers (taint / bid experiments).
